# x/365

`x/365` is a one-year course for an absolute beginner who wants to become a strong candidate for crypto software internships. The specialization is blockchain-based tokenization and real-world assets.

The course combines programming, web development, backend systems, Ethereum, Solidity, smart-contract security, financial operations, public writing, open-source work, and interview preparation.

## The target

After 365 days and about 936 focused hours, you should have:

- four small technical labs that prove specific skills;
- two polished portfolio projects built around a fictional Treasury fund;
- working knowledge of TypeScript, React, Node.js, SQL, Solidity, Foundry, Ethereum, and token standards;
- unit, integration, fuzz, invariant, and end-to-end test evidence;
- clear explanations of fund shares, net asset value, custody, eligibility, issuance, redemption, settlement, and reconciliation;
- public build logs and project demonstrations;
- regular data-structures practice, mock interviews, applications, and professional conversations;
- enough evidence to compete for general software, full-stack crypto, backend crypto, and junior smart-contract internships.

The goal is not to become a senior protocol engineer in one year. The goal is to become an exceptional beginner who can build, test, explain, and defend serious work.

## Weekly commitment

The default schedule is 18 hours:

| Day | Time | Main purpose |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Monday | 2 hours | Lesson and notes |
| Tuesday | 2 hours | Guided exercise |
| Wednesday | 2 hours | Independent implementation |
| Thursday | 2 hours | Testing and debugging |
| Friday | 1 hour | Review and short post |
| Saturday | 6 hours | Project work and weekly release |
| Sunday | 3 hours | Retrospective, interview practice, and career work |

Use a 10-hour maintenance week during exams or illness. Do not try to recover missed hours with an unsafe marathon.

## Rules

- Write and explain the code yourself. AI can tutor, review, and suggest tests, but you must understand every line you publish.
- Use fictional data, local chains, and public testnets. Never accept real investment funds.
- Do not call a project audited, compliant, secure, or production-ready without the evidence that the word requires.
- Never publish private keys, identity documents, private application data, or another person's confidential information.
- Prefer two finished, reviewed projects to six unfinished applications.
- Treat the X posts as proof of work. Do not let the posting streak control the curriculum.
